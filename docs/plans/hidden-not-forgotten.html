Unblock design · Blockers, round 2

Hidden, not forgotten

A task needs room for detail: what's in the way, the steps, the bits you only remember halfway through. That detail must never make the list longer. It needs to stay out of sight until it's useful, without disappearing from memory.

What went wrong in the first version

The first build made each blocker its own task in the list. Two chores with seven blockers between them became nine rows.

What the first build showed Do the dishesBuy dish soapEmpty the dishwasherClear the counterUnclog the sink Take out the trashBuy trash bagsCheck pickup dayRinse the recycling 9 things, and overwhelmed

The rule for every idea below: the list always shows 2. The seven blockers belong to their tasks and only show when they help.

Three ways to do it

Same deck in each. Tick things off and see how each one feels. They can also be mixed.

Idea A

Folded under the task

Each task shows a small meter of what's cleared. Tap it to unfold its blockers right beneath it, and fold them away again.

Everything is one tap away, in the place you'd look.

Unfolded, a task with four blockers is still a wall of five lines.

Idea B

Only the next step

Each task shows one thing: the next thing to clear. The rest wait in line behind it. Tick it and the next one steps up.

You never see more than one small thing per task. The deck idea, applied inside a task.

The order matters. If the next step can't be done today, the task looks stuck.

Idea C

Clear the way, by place

The list stays clean. One quiet line counts what's in the way and opens a shelf grouped by where you'd fix it: at the store, at home, on the phone.

Errands batch themselves: at the store you see soap and bags together.

One more place to go. The link from blocker to task is a small label.

How hidden things stay remembered

Folding things away only works if they come back at the right time. These are the ways a hidden blocker can get your attention again. Any of them can go with any idea above.

The task always shows how much is in the way, even folded.

Questions for you

  1. Which idea feels lightest when you use it? Or which parts of each would you mix? For example, A's fold with B's "next step" shown while it's folded.
  2. Are blockers and steps the same thing to you? The app already has "steps" on a task. "Buy soap" is something that has to happen before; "rinse, wash, dry" is the doing itself. They could be one list or two.
  3. Which way of being reminded would actually reach you, and which would just become more noise?
  4. Can a blocker be dealt on its own? If you ask for a chore, should "Buy dish soap" come up, or only whole tasks?
